Features

Everything RaceStudio does for a kart racer, and some things it doesn't.

Download

Over WiFi or USB, or the MyChron 4's data key

Mychronix walks you through connecting, joins the MyChron's WiFi for you on Linux or finds a MyChron 6 plugged in by USB, and offers new sessions with RaceStudio-style details. Nothing already downloaded is ever overwritten.

Garage

Drivers, karts and their MyChrons

Seat time and race days per driver. MyChrons can move between karts and drivers (handed down to a younger sibling, say) and every session stays with the kart it was recorded on.

Sessions

Everything by race day

Best laps and lap-time sparklines; filter by dates, drivers, tracks and timed laps; search by anything. Import folders of sessions you already have, too.

Analysis

Every view a click away

Laps opens with the session in a few numbers and the corner that cost the most. Channels over time or distance on a shared cursor, the track colored by any channel, splits, histograms, and RPM against speed with your gearing drawn in.

Corners

The way a data team reads them

Each corner from its braking point to the next one's, against the best you've driven it: the speed given up, how the kart slowed, steering, and the time lost. Added up, the best of every corner is the lap you've already proven you can drive.

Map

Your line, corner by corner

Your line through each corner up close, against the best you drove it, colored by braking and lifting, speed, RPM or where time was won and lost. Every lap faintly, to see how consistent the line is.

Compare

Laps, sessions and drivers

Laps lined up by distance with the time gap, and the track colored by where time was won and lost. Or each driver's best lap at a track.

MyChron 6 motion sensor

Mounted any way, nothing to set

Braking and acceleration, bumps, grip used around the lap, trail braking, curbs hit on most laps, and steering corrections when the MyChron sits on the wheel.

Speed without GPS

For MyChrons without it

Worked out from RPM, gearing and rear tire size (and marked wherever it's used), so a MyChron 4 still gets corners and comparisons.

Events and setups

Your season, and what you changed

Sessions grouped by week, track and championship, with a season summary. Setup sheets per session (gearing, tires, pressures, weight, jetting) carry over from the last one and flag what changed.

Tracks and weather

About 5,600 tracks from AiM

AiM's track database with outlines and satellite views, your custom tracks, favorites, and the weather at the track when each session started.

Session notes

What stands out, measured

Lap-time trends and consistency across a session, and a warning when a sensor that has worked before stops recording.

Which MyChrons work

Mychronix only ever reads from your MyChron and data key. It never changes settings, deletes sessions or updates firmware.

MyChronHow it downloadsStatus
MyChron 6, 6TOver the MyChron's own WiFi, or a USB-C data cableWorks
MyChron 5, 5S, 5 2T, 5S 2TOver WiFi, the same wayExpected to work
MyChron 4, 4 2T, 4 660AiM's USB data keyWorks
MyChron 4 with expansionsData keyExpected to work
MyChron 3Serial cableNot yet

"Works" means tested on that MyChron; "expected" means it reads the same files, but nobody has tried one yet. Have one? Share a session.

Install

Version @VERSION@, released @DATE@. Add the Mychronix repository once and new versions arrive with your system's updates.

Fedora

Fedora 36 or newer. Updates come with dnf upgrade or GNOME Software.

1 · Add the repository
sudo curl -fsSL -o /etc/yum.repos.d/mychronix.repo @URL@/mychronix.repo
2 · Install
sudo dnf install mychronix

dnf asks you to accept the Mychronix signing key the first time; its fingerprint is in the status bar below.

Ubuntu, Debian, Mint, Pop!_OS

Ubuntu 22.04, Debian 12, Mint 21 or newer. Updates come with apt upgrade or your software updater.

1 · Add the signing key and repository
sudo install -d /etc/apt/keyrings
curl -fsSL @URL@/mychronix.gpg | sudo tee /etc/apt/keyrings/mychronix.gpg > /dev/null
echo "deb [signed-by=/etc/apt/keyrings/mychronix.gpg] @URL@/deb ./" | sudo tee /etc/apt/sources.list.d/mychronix.list
2 · Install
sudo apt update && sudo apt install mychronix

Mac

Apple Silicon (M1 and newer), macOS 11 or later. With Homebrew, updates come with brew upgrade.

Install with Homebrew
brew install --cask crgklr/mychronix/mychronix

Or download Mychronix-@VERSION@-arm64.dmg and drag Mychronix into Applications. Mychronix isn't signed by Apple yet: the first time it opens, macOS says it couldn't verify it. Click Done, then in System Settings → Privacy & Security click Open Anyway. On a Mac, join the MyChron's WiFi from the menu bar yourself.

Any other Linux

Arch, CachyOS, openSUSE and the rest: anything from 2022 on.

AppImageNo install: make it executable and double-click it. AppImage updaters keep it current. TarballUnpack anywhere and run bin/mychronix.

For a MyChron 6 over USB or a MyChron 4's data key, Linux needs AiM's USB rule once (the .rpm and .deb install it): see the README.

AI race coaching, with your own assistant

Plenty of lap-timing apps sell an "AI coach" by subscription. Mychronix lets you use the AI assistant you already have instead.

Mychronix Performance Engineer

Claude Desktop, Codex, or any app that supports MCP

Turn it on in Settings, press Add to Claude Desktop or Add to Codex, and restart the assistant. It can list sessions, read a session's laps, setup and weather, go corner by corner, compare laps, read any channel and follow your season, with the same numbers as the app's own screens.

Read-only, and off until you turn it on. It can't change your data or talk to a MyChron, and Mychronix sends nothing itself. What the assistant reads goes to the company that runs it, like anything else you share in that chat.

Your data stays yours

No account and no cloud. Everything is on your computer, in a folder called Mychronix in your home folder.

On your computer

Plain files you can back up

Sessions exactly as the MyChron stores them, with their details and setup sheets beside them; drivers, karts, events and settings as small text files. Removing a MyChron or kart only hides it, and deleting a driver asks twice and moves their sessions to the Trash.

What it fetches

Each can be turned off in Settings

  • AiM's track database, from AiM's server (the one RaceStudio 3 uses)
  • Weather from Open-Meteo: only the track's position and the date are sent
  • Whether a newer Mychronix is out, from GitHub, once a day
  • Maps from OpenStreetMap and Esri, cached once viewed

Share your sessions

Every MyChron and accessory records a little differently, and the only way to support one properly is to read real sessions from it.

Wanted: sessions from every kind of MyChron

The 3, 4, 5 and 6 series, especially with lots of sensors and expansions

Expansion boxes, CAN devices, water and exhaust temperatures, lambda, steering, brake pressure, wheel speed, extra GPS: the more that's plugged in, the better. A session with a few laps is plenty. Open an issue and attach the files zipped up:

  • MyChron 5 and 6: the .xrk or .xrz (and its .xri), from RaceStudio 3 or Mychronix
  • MyChron 4: RaceStudio 2's .drk with its .gpk, .rrk and .imd, or Mychronix's .mc4 files
  • MyChron 3: whatever RaceStudio saved

Say which MyChron it is and what's plugged into it. Issues are public, and session files can hold driver and kart names and the track's position, so share only what you're happy to make public.
